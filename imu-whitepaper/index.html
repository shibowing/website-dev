Toward IMU Foundation Models

A White Paper on Cross-Embodiment Inertial Odometry

Paper arXiv soon Code & results Benchmark BibTeX
Twelve training recordings, three per platform, replayed in real time

Almost every robot carries an inertial measurement unit, yet learned inertial odometry is still trained one platform at a time. This white paper asks how far the field is from an IMU foundation model: one set of weights, any robot, no platform label. It draws on a public benchmark, the ten awarded solutions and controlled experiments.

What works

Four ideas that hold across teams

An idea counts as supported only when several teams measured it independently; the dissenting results are kept beside it.

Idea 1

Read tens of seconds, not one window

Bias, mounting tilt and the platform itself are properties of a recording, not of a window.

Context64 s

240 s of a real handheld recording (accelerometer magnitude). Drag to set a context length; the solutions that read at least that much light up.

Idea 2

Replay motion at other speeds, consistently

A replay at rate r multiplies velocity and angular rate by r and the motion part of the specific force by r², and leaves gravity and bias unchanged.

Idea 3

Fit the physics of a recording

What stays constant over a recording, such as accelerometer bias, mounting tilt and rotor drag, is estimated with the equations of motion.

Axis

One-second means of body-frame specific force against velocity on two fast training flights, with the least-squares fit f = −c v.

Idea 4

Draw more windows from the same labels

The labels exist at the full sensor rate, so shifting the window grid by a random offset gives many more training examples at no cost.

The same signal cut on the official grid (grey) and on grids shifted by a random offset (colour).

The hardest seventeen seconds

A fast flight beyond the labelled speeds

No labelled aerial window is faster than about 11 m/s; one test flight reaches 15.7 m/s. Networks that regress speed flatten out; solutions that take the speed from a physical model follow the truth.

Controlled experiments

Testing the conclusions with one reference model

One small reference model (0.86 M parameters, no physical stage), trained in many ways with three seeds each and scored with the official scorer.

aHow much of the recording

Reading 16 s lowers the score by 8% against 64 s; reading one second raises it by 16%.

bData or capacity

Halving the training recordings raises the score by 15%. A model ten times larger is no better.

cA turned sensor

dA platform never seen

Left out of training, a platform is estimated no better than by predicting zero velocity.

eShared model or specialists

Telling the model its platform adds nothing. Four separately trained specialists are better in every seed.

Symbols: the mean of three seeds. Bands, bars and small marks: the three seeds. Score: the official scorer, lower is better; predicting zero velocity everywhere scores 1. Hover a symbol for its values.
